Method & guide

How Canopy turns a photograph into evidence for human review.

From image to indicator

We matched the 40 supplied photos to the coordinate register by exact filename and SHA-256. The site displays precomputed SegFormer B2 outputs. For new photographs, run the Colab analysis and import the results.

The mask groups pixels into trees, other vegetation, sky, buildings and other features. Shelter visibility is recorded by a person.

How the screening rule works

A photo becomes a candidate when tree pixels are at or below the 25th percentile and sky pixels are at or above the 75th percentile. The rule uses all loaded records and requires at least five.

Detection, photo quality and coordinates must pass review before a record becomes an inspection candidate. Use a site visit to assess ground shade and heat exposure.

Where errors occur

Framing, weather and the date of capture affect what appears in the photo. SegFormer has no dedicated playground equipment class. In PG29, parts of a green slide were classified as vegetation.

Save and reproduce

Review notes for the supplied records are saved on this device. Export the CSV for structured records, or save a portable HTML with the photos, masks and notes included. A fresh visit loads the shared dataset.

Using Canopy

  1. Choose a marker or search for a playground. Records without coordinates are available in the list and register.
  2. Switch between the photo, mask, overlay or swipe view. Use the comparison tab to compare up to four sites.
  3. Record detection, photo quality and location checks in the evidence panel.
  4. Share a link to a record on the hosted site. Save portable HTML to share locally imported images and notes.

Import Colab results

Load an analysis CSV to replace the records in this session. Save a portable HTML first if you need a copy of your current work.

Attach images

Select extracted originals, masks and overlays. Files can use the playground ID, image ID or original filename.
